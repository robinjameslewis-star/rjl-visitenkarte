What it does

  • Is this an e-invoice? The tool answers yes or no and names the format, such as XRechnung or ZUGFeRD, with the legal source. It clearly flags ZUGFeRD files in the basic MINIMUM and BASIC WL profiles, and PDFs without embedded invoice data, as not e-invoices.
  • Readable and printable: issuer, recipient, lines, VAT, totals and payment details as a clear page, with the standard's field numbers if you like.
  • Checked against the official rules: the rules of the European standard EN 16931 from the European Committee for Standardization (CEN) and the XRechnung rules from the German Coordination Office for IT Standards (KoSIT) – the same rules the KoSIT validator uses.
  • Findings in plain words: for each finding the level (error, warning or notice), the rule number, and in plain words what is wrong and what the issuer needs to change.
  • Pay by GiroCode: payee, IBAN, amount and reference with copy buttons and as a QR code for your banking app – only with a valid IBAN, an amount in euros and credit transfer; otherwise the tool says why not.
  • Also: check several files at once, save the invoice's attachments, save the validation report as a file.

How it works

  1. Drag the XML file or the ZUGFeRD PDF into the box above, or choose it – or try an example.
  2. Read the answer: e-invoice or not, and in which format.
  3. Look at the findings. If you received the invoice, send the validation report to the issuer; for your own invoice, complete the details in your invoicing software.
  4. Print or save the validation report; pay by GiroCode or with the copied details.
  5. Keep the original file – the view does not replace it.

What is checked and what is not

In short: the tool checks whether all required details are there and fit together – the business rules of the standard. It does not check whether the details are correct, such as the VAT rate.

Each finding has a level: error – the invoice breaks a rule and the issuer should correct it; warning – please check, the invoice may still be in order; notice – for information only.

If the tool finds values in the wrong format, such as a date written the wrong way, it reports a format error. A file with format errors is not an e-invoice. Missing or inconsistent details (business rule breaches), by contrast, are not format errors.

The tool does not check the XML schema, the formal structure of the file in every detail; format errors that only show up there are not detected. A validation does not replace checking the invoice for completeness and accuracy (letter of the German Federal Ministry of Finance of 15 October 2025, para. 35a).

For specialists: the tool checks the EN 16931 business rules (CEN, rule set 1.3.16) and, for an XRechnung, the XRechnung 3.0.2 rules as well (KoSIT, Schematron 2.6.0), taken unchanged from the KoSIT validator configuration of 31 August 2026, with the severity levels set there. XRechnung includes some rules whose names start with PEPPOL. ZUGFeRD profiles without a KoSIT scenario of their own are checked against the EN 16931 rules. Specific rules of other implementations, such as the full Peppol BIS rules, are not checked.

Compared with the KoSIT validator

Before publication, the tool was compared with the validation the KoSIT validator uses – the same rule files, run outside the browser: 6,715 test invoices (the KoSIT test suite, examples and test cases from CEN and KoSIT and deliberately altered versions of them), 13,430 checks, 56,310 messages. Result: the same rule numbers in every case. Where the validation outside the browser fails on invalid values, the tool reports a format error too.

Questions and answers

How do I open an XRechnung?

Drag the XML file into the box above. SmartFormular E-Rechnung lite shows the invoice in readable form straight away – issuer, recipient, lines, VAT, totals and payment details – and you can print it. You need no separate software; the file is not uploaded.

How can I tell whether an invoice is an e-invoice?

Under German VAT law an e-invoice is an invoice in a structured electronic format that allows electronic processing (section 14(1) sentence 3 UStG). Formats that follow the European standard EN 16931, such as XRechnung, are permitted. A PDF without embedded invoice data, or a photo, is not an e-invoice; German VAT law calls it an "other invoice" (section 14.1(2) German VAT Application Decree, UStAE). The tool tells you this for every file and names the source. As of 23 October 2026.

When does the e-invoicing obligation apply?

Since 1 January 2025, e-invoices have been mandatory between businesses in Germany (section 14(2) sentence 2 no. 1 UStG), and all of them must be able to receive them. Transitional rules apply to issuing (section 27(38) UStG): until the end of 2026 an "other invoice" may still be sent – on paper or, if the recipient agrees, as a PDF, for example. Until the end of 2027 this applies to businesses with a prior-year turnover of up to €800,000 and to EDI invoices. From 2028 the e-invoice is the rule between domestic businesses. Small-amount invoices up to €250, tickets and invoices from small businesses can always be other invoices (sections 33, 34 and 34a UStDV). As of 23 October 2026; not tax advice.

Is a ZUGFeRD invoice an e-invoice?

Yes, from ZUGFeRD 2.0.1 – except for the MINIMUM and BASIC WL profiles, which do not contain all invoice details (section 14.1(14) UStAE, as amended by the Federal Ministry of Finance letter of 15 October 2025). The tool reads the data embedded in the PDF, shows profile and version and checks it. The embedded data counts, not the visible PDF.

What is the difference between XRechnung and ZUGFeRD?

An XRechnung is a pure data file (XML) following the European standard EN 16931 with German additional rules; to read it you need software such as this tool. A ZUGFeRD invoice is a PDF that also contains the invoice data as an XML file: people see a familiar invoice image, software reads the data. Both are permitted e-invoices – ZUGFeRD from version 2.0.1 and not in the MINIMUM and BASIC WL profiles. The tool reads both.

The check reports errors – does that mean it is not an e-invoice?

Not necessarily. Missing or inconsistent details (business rule breaches) are not format errors: for VAT purposes, as a rule, the file remains an e-invoice (Federal Ministry of Finance letter of 15 October 2026, paras. 22 and 23). If a mandatory detail under section 14(4) UStG is missing or wrong, however, the invoice does not meet the legal requirements. For each finding the tool shows what is wrong and what the issuer needs to change – send them the validation report. If in doubt, check with your tax adviser.

What do error messages such as BR-DE-15 or BR-CO-26 mean?

They are validation rule numbers. Rules such as BR-CO-26 come from the European standard EN 16931, rules starting with BR-DE from XRechnung, and rules with UBL or CII concern the structure of the file. BR-DE-15, for example, reports a missing buyer reference (the Leitweg-ID for public authorities), BR-CO-26 a missing seller identifier such as the VAT number. Drag the invoice into the box above: the tool explains every message in plain words and says what the issuer needs to change.

How accurate is the check?

It uses the same rules as the KoSIT validator: the EN 16931 business rules from CEN and the XRechnung rules, taken unchanged from the KoSIT configuration. With every new version the results are compared on thousands of test invoices with the validation the KoSIT validator uses; the rule numbers must match. The tool does not check the XML schema, the formal structure of the file, or whether the details are correct, such as the VAT rate.

Are my invoices uploaded?

No. The files are read and checked only in your browser window. Only the tool and the validation rules are loaded from robin.vision; no invoice data goes anywhere. The downloadable file works without any internet connection.

What is a GiroCode?

A QR code for credit transfers following the European Payments Council standard. Many banking apps read it: payee, IBAN, amount and reference are filled in, and you check and send. The tool only shows it if the IBAN is valid, the amount is in euros and above zero, and credit transfer is given as the payment means – otherwise it says why not.
