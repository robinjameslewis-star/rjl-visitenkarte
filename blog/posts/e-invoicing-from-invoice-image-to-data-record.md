You already know it from integrated business software (ERP) and digital HR management with an employee portal (HRMS with ESS): master data and transaction data are made available for further processing as they arise, either in real time or at defined intervals. ERP systems have been doing this for invoicing for decades. The invoicing data is available as a record for further processing, for example in accounts receivable. In this process, the invoice image has never mattered for the result. It was only needed by the invoice recipient and for the tax audit. This last reason for the invoice image to exist disappeared when the changes to German tax law around e-invoicing in *Section 14(1) of the German VAT Act (UStG)*, *Section 147(6) of the German Fiscal Code (AO)* and *para. 76 of the GoBD* came into force on 1 January 2025. I cannot understand why so many still cling to the bureaucratic monster that is the invoice image and spend more time on the remaining transitional rules than on adapting their processes. The call for simplification is always loud – until we have to change our habits to get it. Working consistently with data records instead of invoice images really is a great relief. To share this sense of relief, I would like to show you how laborious our habit of translating invoice images into data records is. For example, we copy IBANs from invoices into our banking software when the payee is not yet available as a data record. That is no longer necessary once we move from invoice image to data record. In my next piece in the series *Surfing the data stream of value creation*, I will look at further examples of how seamless data flow, straight-through processing and interface integration avoid entering the same data twice.
