In my last piece I described where data still changes media – from data record to paper and back. The next question is: how do data get directly where they belong instead?

The answer is interface integration.

Accounting receives data from many sources: invoicing, banking, document receipt, payroll, cash registers. Without direct connections, these data are exported, emailed, downloaded, manually imported, or retyped. Each of these steps is a media break that costs time and produces errors.

The GoBD regulate in paragraphs 66 to 73 what interfaces must look like: documented, logged, with defined error handling. This is not a hurdle but a blueprint.

That the technology works is shown by the figures from DATEV's automation services. The Bank Automation Service generates over 10 million booking suggestions per month for more than 100,000 bookkeeping records. The Invoice Automation Service produces 12 million suggestions from 150,000 records. Both services learn from corrections and steadily improve their accuracy. (Source: DATEV press release, 10 June 2026)

An example: a client uses an ERP system. Their outgoing invoices exist as structured data records there. Instead of exporting them as PDFs, loading them into the document receipt system, and having them read out again, the invoicing interface could transfer the data directly into the accounting software. The detour via PDF and OCR would be eliminated.

The benefit is clear: no more manual matching of bank statements, invoices and payments. Fewer typing errors. Faster month-end closings. And the AI improves with every correction.

The interfaces exist. The GoBD permit them. What is often missing is simply the decision to use them.
