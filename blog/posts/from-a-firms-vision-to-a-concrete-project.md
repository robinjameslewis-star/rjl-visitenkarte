A vision becomes tangible when it is possible to describe what should work differently on an ordinary day. “We want to become more digital” leaves many decisions open. A more specific picture would be this: a new team member can find the information needed for their work, identify their next tasks and see who makes decisions when an exception arises. That is a picture people can work towards.

To develop this kind of vision, I would first bring together three perspectives: the leadership of the tax and accounting firm, the people involved in the daily process and those who need its outcome. What should work reliably? Which interruptions should become less frequent? What information is currently missing when a decision needs to be made? The different answers should be made visible before agreeing on a joint project.

## Build the vision around everyday work

Consider a fictional example: a firm wants to improve onboarding. Its leadership wants a clear view of progress. Experienced team members want to make recurring explanations accessible. New colleagues need understandable tasks and people they can turn to. A shared objective might be: for a defined area of work, the introduction is documented clearly enough for new colleagues to understand their next steps and ask focused questions.

This does not yet determine the technical solution. First, assess what is already in place. Which materials exist? Who maintains them? What has only been explained verbally so far? Which access permissions and decisions are needed? The findings might point to organising existing content, clarifying responsibilities or adding specific instructions. An interactive website or an AI worker could be a later addition.

## Define the first trial

A project becomes manageable when its scope is clear. In this example, the first trial could cover a particular area of work and one stage of onboarding. Other areas would remain outside its scope for now. Record the expected outcome, the people involved, the time available and the decision still to be made. This helps the team understand what the trial is intended to achieve and where its limits lie.

Evaluation also belongs at the beginning. Which questions could be answered using the materials? Where was additional support needed? Was the information clear and up to date? These observations are reviewed together. A trial can be adjusted or stopped if its assumptions do not hold. That is part of a process whose development can be understood and reviewed.

## Prepare the decision

When several ideas emerge, a few criteria can help compare them: their practical value in everyday work, the effort required to prepare and maintain them, the information needed and their dependence on other projects. The decision remains with the firm's leadership. A short written comparison can prepare the ground; a conversation can help where different expectations need to be resolved.

A vision therefore does not need to be a lengthy strategy document. One page describing the desired working day, the current situation and a limited first project is enough to begin. It provides a shared reference point for further decisions.
