Legal framework

The EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) has applied to the AI literacy obligation since 2 February 2025. The Digital Omnibus (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force since 27 July 2026) softened Article 4: providers and deployers must take measures that promote the AI literacy of their staff – a specific level per person is no longer owed. Neither a certificate nor a test nor a format is prescribed; a training record serves as evidence.

Since 2 August 2026 the national market surveillance authorities oversee the obligation; there is no separate fine for breaching it. Advertising a “mandatory course” or threatening fines does not fit that picture, so the workshop is offered for what it is: a measure with evidence.

The workshop explains obligations in general terms, with the legal position stated for each edition. It is not legal advice; for individual cases it refers to tax advisers, lawyers and data protection officers. Questions and answers

Is this training mandatory?

No. Article 4 of the EU AI Act obliges organisations that use AI to take measures that promote the AI literacy of their staff. Which measures is for the organisation to decide; the law prescribes neither a certificate nor an exam. The workshop is one such measure, with evidence so that you can show what you have done.

What is the EU AI Act training?

The documented part of the workshop: each person clicks through the online course and takes the test; on passing, the certificate for the training under Article 4 of the EU AI Act is issued, verifiable online. The participant list serves as evidence for your training records. The workshop beforehand lowers the barriers and supplies the examples from your daily work.

Why does it all start with the workshop and not with the course?

Because questions from your own daily work are what help most – and because barriers fall in conversation, not while reading. In the workshop your team develops its own use cases and builds a small tool with me for a real pain point. The course afterwards consolidates the material in small steps, and the test completes it.

What is the tool we build in the workshop?

A single HTML file that runs in the browser and eases one concrete task of your team – checking a list, building a text by fixed rules, summarising figures from a table. No installation, no data in the cloud. The team experiences how to instruct AI and how to check its results.

What does the employer see?

Each person's status – invited, started, course completed, passed – and the certificate number, as a list for the training records. Scores and the number of attempts are not shown.

How long is the certificate valid?

It states the edition and legal position of the training and can be verified online for three years. Because tools and rules change, there is an annual refresher with a new edition.

What if someone fails the test?

After every submission the correct answer and an explanation are shown for each question. The test can be repeated as often as needed, with freshly drawn questions. Failed attempts are visible only to the person.

What do participants need?

A browser and an email address. Access is set up with a passkey, for example fingerprint or face recognition, without a password. The online course runs in the browser, on a phone too; nothing is installed.

Is the workshop legal advice?

No. It explains obligations in general terms, with the legal position stated for each edition, and refers to tax advisers, lawyers and data protection officers for individual cases.

Are products shown in the workshop?

Yes. I demonstrate tools on real cases: SmartFormular Fibu lite, Fibu and Deklaration by robin.vision – and, over time, products from partner companies that may pay me a fee or a commission for the presentation. Partner offers are labelled as such; the content of the training does not change because of them. What you use is your decision; contracts are made directly with the provider. The terms cover this in Section 8, and the notes for participants mention it too.
